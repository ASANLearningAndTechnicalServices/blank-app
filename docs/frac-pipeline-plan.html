FRAC Pipeline: lead to collected payment, in one place

A business tracker for the sales and accounts teams at FRAC. It shows every new and existing business proposition from first enquiry to won order. After a deal is won it follows three things: order booking, report delivery and billing, and payment collection. It does not price parameters, build quotations or keep the books. It records the business, keeps the customer's files and reads the numbers that LIMS and Tally already hold. It stands on its own and does not depend on any other FRAC tool being ready.

Fill it in under a minuteA new lead needs five fields. The one rule the tool enforces: every open deal has a next step and a date.
Sheets in, nothing retypedUpload the LIMS booking export and the Tally reports. The tool shows what changed and updates its records.
Colour means somethingCalm, bright screens. Colour appears only for state: overdue, at risk, paid. Amounts read as ₹ lakh and crore.
Ask it from Claude or ChatGPTA connector lets the team ask "what do I chase this week?" and log a follow-up from chat.
Scope

What it does, and what it deliberately leaves out

In scope

  • Leads and deals for all five FRAC services: Analytical Testing, Research, Survey / Study, FoSTaC Training, Consultancy
  • New business and existing business (repeat orders, a new service for an existing customer, rate-contract renewals, tenders)
  • Follow-up log and reminders for sales and collections
  • After a deal is won: bookings, report delivery, invoices, payments, ageing and follow-ups
  • A file vault per customer for old and new documents (proposals, POs, agreements, reports, invoices)
  • Sheet uploads from LIMS and Tally that update existing records
  • Dashboards for the weekly sales review and the monthly collections review
  • Read and update access from Claude and ChatGPT

Out of scope

  • Parameters, packages, rates, feasibility and quotation building. A deal stores only the quoted value and the quotation file, however the quotation was prepared.
  • Sample registration and report generation. That stays in LIMS / the booking software; the tracker reads its export.
  • Invoicing and accounting. That stays in Tally; the tracker reads its exports and never writes back.
  • Marketing campaigns, email sequences, call dialling
  • Complex approvals or discount workflows

LIMS and Tally stay the record for their own data. The tracker joins them up by customer and deal. FORT can be linked later, once it is stable, without changing the tracker's design.

Ease of use

Rules that keep data entry light

Five fields for a new lead

Customer (search existing or type a new name), service, new or existing, estimated value, next step with a date. Contact person is optional. Everything else can be added later.

One enforced rule

An open deal cannot be saved without a next step and its date. That single habit drives the Today list, the reminders and the "no next step" alert.

Edit where you look

Change stage, value, owner or date directly in the board or list. No separate edit screen for common changes. Logging a call is one click with a short note.

Customers come pre-loaded

On day one the customer list is imported from the most complete source available: the current tracker, the LIMS customer list or Tally's ledger list. Typing a name suggests the existing customer before creating a duplicate.

Indian formats throughout

₹ in lakh and crore (₹4.8 L, ₹1.84 Cr), dates as 06 Oct 26, financial year April to March, quarters as Q3 FY27, GSTIN on the customer.

Works on a phone

Sales staff can add a lead or log a visit from the field. Today, Pipeline and a customer's files all work at phone width.

Recognises the file

The upload reads the header row and picks the saved layout for that source. A new layout is mapped once by dragging columns onto fields, then saved for everyone.

Shows changes before saving

New, changed, unchanged and needs-attention rows are counted and listed field by field. Nothing is written until someone clicks Apply.

Each field has one owner

Tally owns invoice amounts and pending balances. LIMS owns booking and report facts. People own stages, notes, owners and follow-ups. An upload never overwrites a field it does not own.

Learns customer names

When Tally says "SUNRISE DAIRY PVT. LTD. (HO)", the user picks the customer once and the alias is remembered. GSTIN is used to match when the sheet has it.

Links money to deals

An invoice is linked to a booking by booking no. or PO no. when the sheet carries it, otherwise suggested from the same customer's recent bookings for one-click confirmation.

Keeps a full trail

The raw file and every row are stored exactly as received, before any record is updated. Any batch can be undone for 30 days.

Snapshot rule: a Bills Receivable export is treated as the complete list of open invoices on that date. An invoice that was open last time and is missing now is shown as "will be marked Paid" in the preview. The user can untick any row before applying, so a partial export never closes invoices by mistake.

Architecture

One web app, one database, one connector endpoint

Who uses it
Sales, CRM, leadershipBrowser or phone, signed in with an @fraclabs.org Google account
AccountsUploads Tally exports, updates collections
Claude and ChatGPTCall the connector endpoint with the user's own sign-in (OAuth 2.1)
The portal
Next.js web app on VercelScreens, server actions, sheet parsing (Excel / CSV), and the /mcp connector endpoint, all in one deployment
Daily jobsMorning digest email of follow-ups due; overdue and not-billed alerts
Data
Supabase, Mumbai regionPostgreSQL with row-level security, Google sign-in, OAuth server for the connector, private file storage, daily backups
LIMS and TallyStay as they are. Their Excel exports are uploaded; nothing is written back.

Why this stack, not Streamlit

  • Streamlit Community Cloud does not keep files saved on its disk across restarts. A tracker must keep data and files permanently.
  • Claude and ChatGPT need an always-on HTTPS endpoint with sign-in. Streamlit cannot serve one; Next.js serves the screens and the connector from one codebase.
  • Drag-and-drop boards, inline editing and a phone layout are standard in a React UI and hard to make feel polished in Streamlit.
  • Supabase supplies the database, Google sign-in, the OAuth server the connector needs, and file storage, so there are no extra services to run.

Independent of FORT

  • The tracker has its own customer list and data structure. It does not read FORT's tables or assume how FORT stores data.
  • A deal has a free-text enquiry / quotation reference, so it works with today's quotation process and with FORT later.
  • Once FORT is stable, a small agreed hand-off (customer name or GSTIN, reference no., value, quotation file) can create or advance a deal. Nothing in this plan has to change for that.

Access

Google sign-in limited to the fraclabs.org domain. Roles: Sales, Leadership, Accounts, Admin. Enforced in the database, so the connector cannot see more than the portal.

Data safety

Hosted in India (Mumbai). Private repository; no customer data in git. Daily backups, signed time-limited links for files, full change log.

Running cost

About US$45 a month (≈ ₹4,000): Supabase Pro $25 and one Vercel Pro seat $20. Viewers need no seat. Claude or ChatGPT plans are separate.

Reviews

Two built-in reviews replace the weekly spreadsheet

Weekly sales review

  • Pipeline by stage, by owner and by service, in ₹ and count
  • Weighted forecast by month for the current quarter
  • New vs existing business split
  • Deals won and lost this week, with lost reasons
  • Stale deals: no activity in 14 days
  • Win rate and average days from lead to won

Monthly collections review

  • Booked → reported → billed → collected for the month
  • Reported but not billed (revenue that is waiting on an invoice)
  • Outstanding by age: not due, 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+
  • DSO (days sales outstanding) trend
  • Promises due and promises broken
  • Top 10 outstanding customers with last follow-up

Every table exports to Excel for anyone who still wants the sheet.

Your call

Decisions and inputs needed before building

1. Stack

Recommendation: Next.js + Supabase as above. The alternative is a Streamlit app plus a separate small Python service for the connector and a hosted database; it is quicker to start but harder to make polished and needs two deployments.

2. Who sees what

Recommendation: everyone sees every customer and deal; people edit their own deals. Say if any team needs restricted visibility.

3. The booking software

Which software is used for order booking (LIMS or another)? A sample export with the column headers is needed, including booking no., customer, sample count, value and report status.

4. Tally exports

Samples of the Sales Register and Bills Receivable exports as Accounts produces them today. Confirm that bill-wise details are maintained for customer ledgers, which Bills Receivable depends on.

5. Claude and ChatGPT plans

Which plans FRAC holds. Claude Team or Enterprise and ChatGPT Business or Enterprise allow organisation-wide connectors with write actions; personal plans have limits.

6. File storage

Recommendation: files stored inside the portal (private, searchable, linked to deals), with an optional Google Drive folder link per customer for existing folders. Bulk-import old files by zip.

7. Stage names, lost reasons, users

Confirm or rename the five stages, the service list and the lost reasons; send the list of users with their roles; share the current lead tracker and the best available customer list for the one-time import.
