FRAC Pipeline: lead to collected payment, in one place

A business tracker for the sales and accounts teams at FRAC. It shows every new and existing business proposition from first enquiry to won order. After a deal is won it follows three things: order booking, report delivery and billing, and payment collection. It does not price parameters, build quotations or keep the books. It records the business, keeps the customer's files and reads the numbers that LIMS and Tally already hold. It stands on its own and does not depend on any other FRAC tool being ready.

Leads arrive on their ownWebsite and campaign forms drop straight into a lead inbox. A lead typed by hand needs five fields.
Works like a sheet when you need itAny list opens as a spreadsheet-style grid: filter it, call down it, update status and remarks row by row.
Colour means somethingCalm, bright screens. Colour appears only for state: overdue, at risk, paid. Amounts read as ₹ lakh and crore.
Ask it from Claude or ChatGPTA connector lets the team ask "what do I chase this week?" and log a follow-up from chat.
Scope

What it does, and what it deliberately leaves out

In scope

  • Leads and deals for all five FRAC services: Analytical Testing, Research, Survey / Study, FoSTaC Training, Consultancy
  • New business and existing business (repeat orders, a new service for an existing customer, rate-contract renewals, tenders)
  • Leads captured from the FRAC website and from campaign forms (Google Forms and other form tools) into a lead inbox
  • Import of the Google Sheets trackers that individuals keep today
  • Spreadsheet-style grid editing with filters, saved lists and a calling mode for working through a list
  • Follow-up log and reminders for sales and collections
  • After a deal is won: orders, report status, invoices, payments, ageing and follow-ups
  • A file vault per customer for old and new documents (proposals, POs, agreements, reports, invoices)
  • Sheet uploads from LIMS and Tally that update existing records
  • Dashboards for the weekly sales review and the monthly collections review
  • Read and update access from Claude and ChatGPT

Out of scope

  • Parameters, packages, rates, feasibility and quotation building. A deal stores only the quoted value and the quotation file, however the quotation was prepared.
  • Sample registration and report generation. That stays in LIMS / the booking software; the tracker reads its order and report status sheets.
  • Invoicing and accounting. That stays in Tally; the tracker reads its exports and never writes back.
  • Marketing campaigns, email sequences, call dialling
  • Complex approvals or discount workflows

LIMS and Tally stay the record for their own data. The tracker joins them up by customer and deal. FORT can be linked later, once it is stable, without changing the tracker's design.

No overlap with FORT

The tracker covers the commercial side: who the business is with, what it is worth, what happens next and whether it has been paid. Anything technical or operational about an enquiry is left to FORT, as FORT is planned today.

Lead capture

Every lead lands in one inbox, wherever it came from

Website and campaign leads arrive without anyone typing them. Each one is checked for duplicates, assigned to an owner and shown in the lead inbox until someone converts it to a deal, adds it to an existing customer or discards it with a reason.

Website leads come straight here, with no Google Sheet in between. A submission reaches the tracker within seconds, is assigned the moment it arrives, and the owner is alerted straight away. Google Forms work the same way through a small script on the form.

Website

FRAC website enquiry forms

Each form gets its own secure address in the tracker. The website's existing form posts to it, or a small embed snippet replaces the form. The page, and the campaign tags in the link (source, medium, campaign), are saved with the lead.

Campaigns

Google Forms

A short script attached to the form sends each response to the tracker the moment it is submitted. Reading the response sheet every 15 minutes is kept only as a fallback for a form that cannot take the script. Choose the campaign once and every response is tagged with it.

Campaigns

Other form tools and ads

Typeform, Jotform, Zoho Forms and similar tools post to the same secure address. LinkedIn and Facebook lead ads, which have no direct webhook, connect through Zapier, Make or n8n.

Later

Forms built in the tracker

A simple form builder for events: pick fields, name the campaign, share the link or a QR code at an exhibition or a FoSTaC session.

What happens to each submission

  • Spam check: an invisible challenge (Cloudflare Turnstile), a hidden trap field and a limit per sender. Flagged entries go to a Spam tab, not the inbox.
  • Duplicate check: email, phone, GSTIN and company name are compared with existing customers and open leads. A likely match is offered as "Add to Metro Bakers".
  • Acknowledgement (optional): an automatic thank-you email to the enquirer with the name of the person who will call.
  • Backup: the website's existing email notification stays on for the first few weeks, so no lead can be missed while the new route beds in.

Allocated in seconds, contacted fast

  • On arrival: assigned by rules (service, region, campaign; an existing customer goes to its account owner), otherwise turn by turn among people on duty. People on leave are skipped.
  • Instant alerts: an in-app alert, a push notification on phone or desktop, and an email. A WhatsApp alert is optional and needs a WhatsApp Business API provider, charged per message.
  • Response clock: each new lead shows how long it has waited until a call or email is logged. If nobody has made contact within 30 minutes in working hours (adjustable), the Sales Head is alerted and can reassign it in one click.
  • Out of hours: the acknowledgement goes at once; the lead goes to the morning roster and tops that person's Today list.

Campaigns are measured end to end

Each form belongs to a campaign: a name, channel and dates, with spend if you want cost per lead. Because the lead becomes a deal and the deal reaches payment, the campaign report runs all the way through.

Large lists

A spreadsheet-style grid for working through long lists

The board and forms suit one deal at a time. Collections calls, cleaning up imported leads or a campaign call list need rows and columns. Every list in the tracker (leads, deals, customers, invoices) can switch to a grid view.

Feels like Excel

Click a cell to edit it. Status and owner cells are dropdowns, dates open a calendar. Arrow keys, Tab and Enter move around. Copy and paste blocks of cells to and from Excel or Google Sheets, fill down, and paste many rows at once to add new leads; invalid cells are highlighted, not rejected silently.

Filter, then save the list

Sort, filter, group, hide and freeze columns. Save the result as a named list, personal or shared, such as "Overdue over 30 days · North" or "FoSTaC Oct leads not yet called". Counts and totals update as people work.

Calling mode

Open a saved list and work through it one customer at a time: contact number, invoices, past remarks, status buttons, promised amount and date, next call. Save & next moves to the following row.

Remarks are a history

A new status or remark is added with the date and the person's name. The grid shows the latest one; the full history is one click away. Nothing anyone wrote is overwritten.

Several people, one list

Changes appear for everyone straight away. A row someone else is editing shows their initials, so two people do not call the same customer.

Bulk changes and export

Select rows and reassign the owner, set a next call date or change a status in one step. Export any filtered list to Excel.

Built on Glide Data Grid (free, MIT licence), which handles hundreds of thousands of rows with editing, multi-cell selection and copy-paste. The common alternative, AG Grid, keeps copy-paste and range selection in its paid edition.

Ease of use

Rules that keep data entry light

Five fields for a new lead

Customer (search existing or type a new name), service, new or existing, estimated value, next step with a date. Contact person is optional. Everything else can be added later.

One enforced rule

An open deal cannot be saved without a next step and its date. That single habit drives the Today list, the reminders and the "no next step" alert.

Edit where you look

Change stage, value, owner or date directly in the board or list. No separate edit screen for common changes. Logging a call is one click with a short note.

Customers come pre-loaded

On day one the customer list is imported from the most complete source available: the current tracker, the LIMS customer list or Tally's ledger list. Typing a name suggests the existing customer before creating a duplicate.

Indian formats throughout

₹ in lakh and crore (₹4.8 L, ₹1.84 Cr), dates as 06 Oct 26, financial year April to March, quarters as Q3 FY27, GSTIN on the customer.

Works on a phone

Sales staff can add a lead or log a visit from the field. Today, Pipeline and a customer's files all work at phone width.

Recognises the file

The upload reads the header row and picks the saved layout for that source. A new layout is mapped once by dragging columns onto fields, then saved for everyone.

Shows changes before saving

New, changed, unchanged and needs-attention rows are counted and listed field by field. Nothing is written until someone clicks Apply.

Each field has one owner

Tally owns invoice amounts and pending balances. The order sheet owns order and sample details; the report status sheet owns report progress. People own stages, notes, owners and follow-ups. An upload never overwrites a field it does not own.

Learns customer names

When Tally says "SUNRISE DAIRY PVT. LTD. (HO)", the user picks the customer once and the alias is remembered. GSTIN is used to match when the sheet has it.

Links money to deals

An invoice is linked to an order by order no. or PO no. when the sheet carries it, otherwise suggested from the same customer's recent orders for one-click confirmation.

Keeps a full trail

The raw file and every row are stored exactly as received, before any record is updated. Any batch can be undone for 30 days.

Importing personal Google Sheets

  • The person picks the sheet with Google's own file picker. The tracker can open only the files picked this way, nothing else in their Drive.
  • Columns are mapped once. Free-text statuses such as "Hot", "Follow up" or "Done" are mapped to stages once per sheet.
  • Amounts written as 2.5L, 2,50,000 or ₹2.5 lakh, and dates in mixed formats, are read correctly. Blank and heading rows are skipped.
  • Columns with no matching field are kept as notes on the record, not dropped.
  • The same customer in two people's sheets is flagged so it is merged, not duplicated. The sheet's owner becomes the deal owner unless changed.

After the import

The sheet is marked with the import date, and from an agreed date it becomes read-only for reference. Keeping it in use would mean two versions of the truth. Anyone who still prefers rows and columns gets the same feel in the tracker's grid view.

Google access runs through an app registered as Internal in FRAC's Google Workspace, so only fraclabs.org accounts can use it.

Order and report status: two sheets, one key

  • Both sheets carry the same order no., so they are uploaded separately, whenever each is ready, and meet on the same order.
  • An order with several sample names arrives as several rows with the same order no.; the tracker groups them into one order with one line per sample name, e.g. Ghee × 6 and Butter × 6.
  • No sample IDs are needed. Report progress is counted per order, or per sample name when the report sheet gives it.
  • A report row for an order not uploaded yet is held, shown under Needs attention, and applied automatically when the order arrives.
  • A report row naming a sample the order does not contain is flagged rather than guessed.

Snapshot rule: a Bills Receivable export is treated as the complete list of open invoices on that date. An invoice that was open last time and is missing now is shown as "will be marked Paid" in the preview. The user can untick any row before applying, so a partial export never closes invoices by mistake.

Why this stack, not Streamlit

  • Streamlit Community Cloud does not keep files saved on its disk across restarts. A tracker must keep data and files permanently.
  • Claude and ChatGPT need an always-on HTTPS endpoint with sign-in. Streamlit cannot serve one; Next.js serves the screens and the connector from one codebase.
  • Drag-and-drop boards, inline editing and a phone layout are standard in a React UI and hard to make feel polished in Streamlit.
  • Supabase supplies the database, Google sign-in, the OAuth server the connector needs, and file storage, so there are no extra services to run.

Independent of FORT

  • The tracker has its own customer list and data structure. It does not read FORT's tables or assume how FORT stores data.
  • A deal has a free-text enquiry / quotation reference, so it works with today's quotation process and with FORT later.
  • Once FORT is stable, a small agreed hand-off (customer name or GSTIN, reference no., value, quotation file) can create or advance a deal. Nothing in this plan has to change for that.

Access

Google sign-in limited to the fraclabs.org domain. Roles: Sales, Leadership, Accounts, Admin. Enforced in the database, so the connector cannot see more than the portal.

Data safety

Hosted in India (Mumbai). Private repository; no customer data in git. Daily backups, signed time-limited links for files, full change log.

Running cost

About US$45 a month (≈ ₹4,000): Supabase Pro $25 and one Vercel Pro seat $20. Viewers need no seat. Turnstile spam protection is free. Claude or ChatGPT plans, and Zapier or Make for ad forms, are separate.

Portable from day one

A Docker setup ships with the code, the database is standard PostgreSQL, and files sit behind a storage layer that works with Supabase or any S3-compatible bucket. Supabase itself can be self-hosted. Moving from managed hosting to FRAC's own server later means copying the database and files and changing settings, not rewriting the app.

Test and live copies

A test copy is set up with the Foundation module, so each module can be reviewed on a real web address. The live copy, with the team's real data, opens at rollout. Both run from the same repository.

Must be on the public internet

Website forms and the Claude and ChatGPT connector reach the tracker over the internet, so it cannot sit only on the office network. It gets an address such as pipeline.fraclabs.org (one DNS record), HTTPS, and sign-in limited to fraclabs.org accounts.

Reviews

Two built-in reviews replace the weekly spreadsheet

Weekly sales review

  • Pipeline by stage, by owner and by service, in ₹ and count
  • Weighted forecast by month for the current quarter
  • New vs existing business split
  • Leads by source and campaign, conversion, and time to first response
  • Deals won and lost this week, with lost reasons
  • Stale deals: no activity in 14 days
  • Win rate and average days from lead to won

Monthly collections review

  • Booked → reported → billed → collected for the month
  • Reported but not billed (revenue that is waiting on an invoice)
  • Outstanding by age: not due, 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+
  • DSO (days sales outstanding) trend
  • Promises due and promises broken
  • Top 10 outstanding customers with last follow-up

Every table exports to Excel for anyone who still wants the sheet.

What sets the pace

  • FRAC's review of each module, ideally within a day
  • Accounts created and keys added to the environment (Claude cannot sign up for services or click through their dashboards)
  • Real sample sheets, so imports are built on actual columns
  • Website changes, if the site is run by an outside agency

How each module is checked

  • Automated tests for the business rules: assignment, duplicate matching, upload changes, the Tally snapshot rule
  • The screens driven in a real browser at desktop and phone width
  • A short note of what was built, what was tested and what is still open, before FRAC reviews it

Build times are Claude's estimates of focused working time, not measured yet. Later: forms built in the tracker with QR codes, hand-off from FORT once it is finished, a direct read from Tally, WhatsApp reminders to customers.

Your call

Decisions and inputs needed before building

1. Stack

Recommendation: Next.js + Supabase as above. The alternative is a Streamlit app plus a separate small Python service for the connector and a hosted database; it is quicker to start but harder to make polished and needs two deployments.

2. Who sees what

Recommendation: everyone sees every customer and deal; people edit their own deals. Say if any team needs restricted visibility.

3. Order and report status sheets

A sample of each export with its column headers. Order sheet: order no., order date, customer, sample name, number of samples, value. Report status sheet: order no., sample name if available, samples reported, status, report date.

4. Tally exports

Samples of the Sales Register and Bills Receivable exports as Accounts produces them today. Confirm that bill-wise details are maintained for customer ledgers, which Bills Receivable depends on.

5. Claude and ChatGPT plans

Which plans FRAC holds. Claude Team or Enterprise and ChatGPT Business or Enterprise allow organisation-wide connectors with write actions; personal plans have limits.

6. File storage

Recommendation: files stored inside the portal (private, searchable, linked to deals), with an optional Google Drive folder link per customer for existing folders. Bulk-import old files by zip.

7. Website and campaign forms

Which platform runs the FRAC website (WordPress or another), which tools campaigns use for forms, and how new leads should be assigned: by service, by region, by campaign or turn by turn.

8. Personal sheets

A list of the Google Sheets people keep today and who owns each, and an agreed date after which they become read-only.

9. Stage names, lost reasons, users

Confirm or rename the five stages, the service list and the lost reasons; send the list of users with their roles; share the current lead tracker and the best available customer list for the one-time import.
